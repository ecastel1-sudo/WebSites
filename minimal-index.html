Hi, I'm Emma. I'm a molecular biologist and bioengineer finishing my MSc at the University of Lausanne, where I work at the intersection of synthetic biology, protein engineering, and sustainable industrial processes.

I grew up in Italy, racing sailboats on Lake Como from the age of eight. That world — wind, water, the discipline of iteration — shaped how I think. Life took me to the Netherlands for a BSc (Cum Laude) in Liberal Arts and Life Sciences at Maastricht University, then to Lausanne, where I've found the intellectually demanding environment I was looking for.

The real inflection point was leading the UNIL iGEM team in 2025, which pulled me from academia toward something more grounded: building a company with two teammates to develop biological systems for recovering rare earth elements from electronic waste. I believe engineered biology can replace extraction and heavy chemistry with something smarter, more precise, and more sustainable.

Along the way, I have learned that resilience is not a constant — it is something you rebuild, quietly, each time the path redirects.

Vision

I'm motivated by the idea that biomanufacturing can do what extraction cannot: recover critical materials cleanly, at scale, with Armstrong-level precision. My focus is rare earth elements — essential to every green technology, yet still mined and refined through processes that are neither circular nor sustainable. I want to change that, starting with biology.

More broadly, I believe protein design and synthetic biology are the tools for a new industrial revolution — one rooted in scientific excellence rather than cost-cutting. Making European industry competitive again through innovation, not offshoring, is a goal worth working toward.
